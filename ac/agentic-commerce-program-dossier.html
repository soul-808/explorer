1.2

The compression model, five questions

Every protocol, vendor and lawsuit in this space answers one of five questions. Learn the five and any new announcement can be placed without memorising a vendor list. Roughly 80 percent of announcements address question one or three, because two and five require somebody to accept liability.

QuestionWhat it meansStatus
1 · FindHow does an agent know your product exists. Agents do not browse, they read. Structured feeds, schema markup, identifiers, merchant run MCP servers.Where the value is today. A data quality problem and therefore fixable.
2 · IntentWhat did the human actually authorise. A human at checkout is the authorisation. An agent needs a portable, provable artifact of delegated authority.The genuinely hard problem and the real frontier.
3 · TrustIs this agent legitimate. Answered cryptographically. Agents sign requests, merchants verify against a key directory.Technically solved. Adoption spreading at the edge.
4 · PayHow does money move without exposing a card. Every rail converged on the same primitive: a credential scoped to one merchant, one amount, minutes of validity.Solved. Multiple competing implementations.
5 · FixWhen it goes wrong, who pays. Dispute frameworks assume a human decided.Unsolved. This is what caps autonomy.

The mandate chain, the idea worth stealing

AP2's contribution is conceptual. Authorisation becomes a signed, portable, verifiable artifact. An intent mandate proves the user delegated this authority. A cart mandate locks exact items, price, merchant and timestamp, which prevents substitution and price manipulation. A payment mandate binds the payment method to that cart, and it is the thing the network sees. The result is a non repudiable audit trail, which is what converts an agent purchase from an unpriceable risk into an underwritable one. That is the entire reason the networks backed it.

1.6

Reality check

Both sets of numbers below are correct. They measure different stages. Conflating them is the most common error in this space.

INFLUENCE, large and converting well EXECUTION the industry markets execution, the money is in influence
Working, strong evidence
  • AI referred traffic up 393 percent year over year in Q1 2026, up 693 percent over the 2025 holidays.
  • That traffic engages 12 percent more, spends 48 percent longer, generates 37 percent higher revenue per visit.
  • Conversion swung from 38 percent worse than direct to 42 percent better in twelve months.
  • Salesforce estimated AI influenced more than 20 percent of global online retail sales in the 2025 holidays.
  • Amazon's Rufus drove roughly 12 billion dollars incremental annualised sales in 2025. Verified
Not working, equally strong evidence
  • Accenture, 25,590 consumers, 16 countries: 74 percent would let an agent handle routine tasks, 32 percent the final decision before payment, only 12 percent autonomous decisions at the payment stage.
  • Forrester mid 2026: very few consumers allow agents to complete purchases without direct oversight, and even fewer do so regularly.
  • Retail sites still are not machine readable, per Adobe's own finding.
  • Gartner projects a large share of agentic projects cancelled by 2027.
  • In surface checkout has underperformed click out wherever it has been measured.

Claims to treat as unproven

Agents will autonomously buy groceries. A one trillion dollar market by 2030, which assumes autonomy that does not exist. Integrate checkout now or die, when the leader just reversed. Crypto rails are winning agent payments, where transaction counts are not demand. One protocol will win outright.

How to read any adoption statistic

Survey results swing sixty points on wording. Would you let AI shop for you returns roughly 70 percent yes. Would you let it complete payment unsupervised returns roughly 12 percent. Before quoting a number, complete this sentence: this is N units of exactly what, measured by whom, among which population, at which funnel stage, compared against what baseline. If you cannot fill every slot, do not use the number.

1.7

Economics and value capture

The organising question is who captures the margin when the buyer is a machine. Two structural shifts matter for planning.

Attribution is breaking

The roughly 13 billion dollar US affiliate industry runs on cookies, clicks and last touch attribution. Agents follow no affiliate links, carry no cookies and travel no referral paths. The classic fourteen click journey compresses to one or two interactions. Replacements are emerging, including platform transaction fees, outcome based commissions tied to incremental sales, and crediting the data source that informed the decision. None is standard yet.

The finding that should concern anyone with a retail media plan

Research reported out of Columbia and Yale found sponsored tags reduce an agent's probability of selecting a product by 11 to 21 percent across models. Reported Agents penalise paid placement because they optimise the user's stated goal rather than platform yield. If that holds, placement based monetisation does not port to agentic surfaces. It has to become performance based. This is the single most important finding for a commerce media strategy, and it is the reason offer eligibility has to be presented as constraint satisfaction rather than as an ad.

The stack from where we sit

Published research is written from the merchant and platform side. Card networks get a line, issuers get none. This is the same field, redrawn around the one band we control outright.

Fig 2.1 · Agentic commerce stack, issuer view

Two contested zones sit either side of our band. Funding choice, because the agent picks the credential and the cardholder no longer reaches for anything. Dispute liability, because Regulation Z puts the consumer facing obligation on us whether or not the network writes agent rules.

2.2

The five questions as outcomes

The same model stated as things that must be true. Each column is a gate. Fail one and the purchase does not happen.

Fig 2.2 · Outcome pillars and their components

Findable means the agent can see what we offer without a human interpreting it. Authorised means we can prove what the customer actually said yes to. Verified means we know the software on the other end is what it claims. Fundable means money moves without a card number reaching a model. Recoverable means when it goes wrong somebody already knows who eats it. The middle three are finished, which is why every vendor announcement lands there. The two bookends are the work, and both require somebody to accept liability.

2.3

The issuer inversion

The five questions do not translate cleanly to a bank. Two of them invert, and those two are where the opportunity is.

QuestionMerchant answerIssuer answerConsequence for us
1 · FindFeed quality, schema, catalog freshnessWe have no catalog. Our equivalent is card value expressed as machine readable constraints: which benefits, protections and offers apply to this specific cartToday this lives in terms and conditions and marketing copy, which is invisible to an agent. This is a new service.
2 · IntentReceive and honour a mandateWe own enrollment, scoping and revocation. Issuer enrollment was the gating step in the Agent Pay pilotThe only column with no external dependency. It ships first.
3 · TrustVerify agent signatures at the edgeLargely inherited from the network key directoryIntegration work, not invention.
4 · PayAccept a scoped tokenMint and bind the token, then price the risk. Mandates convert an agent purchase from unpriceable to underwritableUnderwriting is the franchise. The advantage accrues to whoever holds the evidence.
5 · FixAbsorb the chargeback by defaultRegulation Z means the consumer dispute obligation never left us. The same event is a chargeback problem for one party and a compliance obligation for the otherA product, not a policy memo. Amex moved first with purchase protection.
In an agent world a rewards engine stops being a consumer marketing asset and becomes machine readable eligibility.

The collision worth naming: the sponsored tag penalty finding says agents discount paid placement. A merchant funded offer presented as sponsored gets penalised. The same offer presented as this card lowers the total by eight percent here is constraint satisfaction, and the agent rewards it. Same asset, opposite outcome, entirely a framing and format decision. That decision belongs to this program, not to a marketing team downstream.

2.4

Where we already sit

Real advantages
  • Among the first two issuers to give cardholders Agent Pay access in the September 2025 pilot. Issuer enrollment was the gating step and we shipped it.
  • Agentic commerce is on the record at Investor Day as a strategic priority, with a concrete example of an agent rebooking a cancelled flight on the card with the best travel protections.
  • Arc gives the bank an internal platform for building and deploying agents at enterprise scale.
  • The Kard acquisition brings a commerce media and rewards platform built on verified transaction data and merchant funded offers, which is the raw material for offer eligibility.
Real exposure
  • The agent picks the funding source. Top of wallet becomes an API question rather than a wallet question, and we have no incumbency in an API.
  • Pressure falls on pricing power more than on the rails. The bank risks supplying the balance sheet under a protocol it does not control.
  • No US federal framework governs agent initiated purchases, and our dispute obligation is unchanged regardless.
  • Prompt injection is structurally unsolved, which caps how far autonomy can go no matter how good the models get.

3.5

Primary flow, human not present purchase

The hardest case, because nobody is at the keyboard when money moves. Human present flows are a strict subset with step six replaced by a live approval.

  1. EXT-01 to EDG-01, IF-01Cardholder enrolls an agent. Authenticates in the bank app, selects an agent, sets caps, merchant categories and an expiry. Step up authentication above the product policy threshold.
  2. APP-01 to EXT-03Bank requests an Agentic Token. Enablement calls Mastercard to mint a token bound to the agent identifier, merchant scope and consent policy. The account number never leaves the CDE.
  3. APP-11 to DAT-02, IF-13Consent is written to the ledger. Append only, with the consent receipt identifier that will appear on every downstream artifact.
  4. EXT-01 to EXT-02Cardholder states an intent. The agent captures goal and constraints and produces a signed intent mandate. The bank sees the mandate, not the conversation.
  5. EXT-02 to EDG-02, IF-02Agent presents itself. Request headers signed under RFC 9421, verified against the network key directory before any business logic runs.
  6. EXT-02 internalConditions are met and the cart locks. The agent generates a cart mandate carrying exact items, price, merchant and timestamp, signed under the earlier intent mandate.
  7. EXT-05 to EXT-04 to EDG-04, IF-04Authorization arrives. The message carries the agent indicator, token assurance level and the mandate reference.
  8. EDG-04 to APP-04 to APP-03, IF-08, IF-09Decision inside the latency budget. Fraud scores with agent aware features. Limits validates cap, velocity and merchant scope against the mandate. Any mismatch declines with a distinct reason code.
  9. APP-08 to EXT-01Cardholder is told. Push notification within seconds showing what the agent bought, with one tap to dispute.
  10. APP-06 to DAT-05, IF-15Evidence is sealed. Mandate chain, agent identity, action log, approved cart and delivery proof written once and retained.
  11. APP-07 to EXT-03, IF-14If disputed, the pack is already built. Regulation Z timers run from claim intake. Evidence assembly is a lookup, not an investigation.
3.6

Architecture decisions

Recorded so the drawing can be read without the meeting that produced it. Each records what was rejected, because that is the part that gets relitigated.

AD-01 Mandate vault is a new system of record
  • A signed artifact authorising a future purchase has no home in the estate.
  • Rejected: mandates as blobs on the consent record. Retrieval and retention obligations differ.
  • Consequence: a new HSM backed store with its own retention, dual control and examination posture.
AD-02 Agent traffic terminates in its own gateway
  • Signature verification, rate limiting and agent reputation are unlike consumer app traffic.
  • Rejected: extending the open banking gateway. Different auth model, different blast radius.
  • Consequence: separately deployable and separately disableable.
AD-03 Decisioning stays in the authorization path
  • Scope enforcement after the fact is reconciliation, not control.
  • Rejected: asynchronous post auth validation with reversal. Poor customer outcome, weak examination story.
  • Consequence: an 80 ms budget for fraud and 25 ms for limits, which constrains model complexity.
AD-04 Offer eligibility is a service, not a report
  • An agent needs cart level card value before checkout, not statement level value after.
  • Rejected: exposing the rewards batch. Wrong latency, wrong granularity, wrong consumer.
  • Consequence: a new low latency service fronting the rewards platform with a public facing contract.
AD-05 Evidence is captured on every agent order
  • Backfilling evidence after the first dispute wave is not possible.
  • Rejected: capture on dispute only. The artifacts are ephemeral at the agent surface.
  • Consequence: storage and retention cost from day one for transactions that will mostly never be disputed.
AD-06 Network agnostic internal model
  • Mastercard Agent Pay is the launch partner, not the internal data model.
  • Rejected: modelling on Agentic Token semantics directly. Locks the estate to one network.
  • Consequence: an internal mandate and scope model with adapters per network. Slower to launch, cheaper to extend.
3.7

Cross cutting concerns and non functional requirements

Security
  • Zero trust service mesh, mutual TLS between all internal containers, workload identity.
  • Prompt injection is treated as an external threat with no internal mitigation. Controls are deterministic: caps, allow lists, mandate binding.
  • Agent credentials are scoped, short lived and revocable in real time from the bank app.
  • Key material in HSM. Dual control on mandate vault access. Privileged access reviewed quarterly.
Availability and performance
  • Authorization path 99.99 percent, active active across two regions.
  • Agent gateway 99.9 percent. Degradation declines new agent orders rather than failing open.
  • Offer eligibility 99.5 percent. Timeout returns no offer rather than blocking checkout.
  • Global kill switch at product and agent level, effective within 60 seconds.
Data and retention
  • Mandates retained for the statute of limitations on the underlying claim, minimum seven years.
  • Audit archive write once, examination ready, indexed by consent receipt.
  • No conversation content ingested. We store mandates and outcomes, not the prompt.
  • Right to delete constrained by record keeping obligations, documented in the privacy assessment.
Observability
  • Distributed tracing from agent request through authorization, correlated on consent receipt.
  • Decline reason codes distinguish scope breach, cap breach, expired mandate and fraud.
  • Unmandated spend on an agent enabled account is a page, not a ticket.

3.9

Scope, assumptions and open decisions

In scope, release one
  • Credit card products only, human present agent purchases.
  • Mastercard Agent Pay as the sole network integration.
  • Enrollment, consent, limits, evidence capture and alerting.
Out of scope
  • Debit and deposit funding sources.
  • Human not present execution, gated behind AD-03 latency proving and a written network dispute rule.
  • Commercial and small business cards.
  • Third party agent access to offer eligibility.
Assumptions
  • Mastercard continues to operate the agent key directory and registry.
  • Card core can carry an agent indicator within one quarter of release train capacity.
  • Merchant side agent verification adoption continues to spread at the edge.
Open decisions
  • Whether we write a voluntary liability commitment for registered agent errors ahead of network rules.
  • Mandate retention period, pending legal opinion.
  • Whether offer eligibility is exposed to agents directly or only through the network.
  • Dual protocol support for a second network, and on what trigger.

A.1

How this was researched, and how to refresh it

Included so the document can be updated rather than rebuilt, and so anyone can check the work. The single highest value step is the third sweep.

The four sweeps
  • 1 Landscape. Broad queries to harvest vocabulary, not answers. You cannot search a field you cannot name. Treat everything here as a lead.
  • 2 Primary. Specs, repositories, filings, earnings, court records. Never take technical detail from a summary when a spec exists.
  • 3 Adversarial. Search for how it failed. Criticism, deprecations, lawsuits, reversals. This is the sweep that earns its keep and the one everyone skips.
  • 4 Recency. Date bounded queries confirming nothing from sweeps one to three has been superseded.
  • 5 Resolve. Query contradictions directly. Report the resolution, never the average.
What skipping sweep three costs
  • Sweeps one and two on this topic produce a confident, well sourced, internally consistent picture in which ChatGPT Instant Checkout is the leading surface and merchants should integrate now.
  • That picture is wrong in its central claim and actively harmful as advice.
  • One adversarial query surfaces the March 2026 retreat and inverts the conclusion.
  • Consensus laundering is invisible from the inside. That is exactly why the sweep is mandatory.

Number hygiene, six traps

Count against value, where transaction counts inflate trivially at near zero marginal cost. Survey wording, which moves results by tens of points. Stage conflation, the most consequential, where influence numbers are reported and execution is implied. Growth rate without a base. Comparison class drift, where better than what changes silently between paragraphs. Projection laundering, where a forecast from an interested party gets cited until it reads as measurement.

A.2

Durability map

Confidence and durability are different axes. Tagging only confidence tells you what to trust but not what to re check, which is how well researched documents rot invisibly. Aim retrieval and refresh at the top two layers. If you can re derive a fact from a framework, do not memorise the fact.

LayerHalf lifeWhat sits here
L0Years to decadesDelegated authority must be provable before anyone lets software spend. A model cannot separate instructions from data, so autonomy has a ceiling. Liability determines autonomy. Whoever controls demand captures the margin. Machines read structured data, and ambiguity a human infers is invisible to an agent.
L1One to three yearsThe five questions. The seven layer stack. The scoped ephemeral credential as the universal payment primitive. Influence against execution. Human present against human not present. The issuer inversion at 2.3.
L2One to four quartersUCP won governance and ACP narrowed to a spec looking for a surface. Discover in the AI, transact on the merchant. Card networks defended successfully. Placement based retail media is under structural threat on agentic surfaces.
L3Weeks to monthsThe 4 percent fee, 393 percent traffic growth, 12 percent payment trust, fewer than 15 merchants live, the 3 times conversion gap, every date, every tool name, every version number, and every latency budget in Part 3.
The conflict rule
  • When a new fact contradicts a principle, the principle wins until the contradiction is explained.
  • One anomaly means check the measurement. Three in the same direction means the framework is the suspect, not the data.
  • The failure mode is dismissing the third, fourth and fifth anomaly with the reasoning that worked on the first.
Trigger events, refresh immediately
  • A major player reverses or kills a flagship product.
  • A governance body changes membership.
  • A significant court ruling or regulatory action.
  • A network writes agentic dispute rules. This one changes our roadmap directly.
  • Somebody tells you something about the field that surprises you. Surprise is data.
A.3

Glossary

Grouped rather than alphabetised. Enough to be fluent in a meeting.

Protocols and standards
  • ACP Agentic Commerce Protocol. OpenAI, Stripe, Meta. Checkout sessions plus delegated payment.
  • UCP Universal Commerce Protocol. Google, Shopify. Capability negotiation across the full lifecycle. The default.
  • AP2 Agent Payments Protocol. Signed mandate chain proving delegated authority.
  • MCP Model Context Protocol. Standard interface for agents to call tools and read data.
  • A2A Agent to agent negotiation and task delegation.
  • Visa TAP Cryptographic agent identity in signed HTTP headers.
  • RFC 9421 HTTP Message Signatures, the spec underneath TAP and Web Bot Auth.
  • x402 HTTP 402 based stablecoin payments for agents and APIs.
Authority and consent
  • Mandate A cryptographically signed record of what a user authorised.
  • Intent mandate Signed statement of goal and constraints.
  • Cart mandate Signed lock on exact items, price, merchant and timestamp.
  • Payment mandate Credential binding the payment method to a specific cart. What the network sees.
  • Human present The user reviews and approves the exact cart before payment.
  • Human not present The agent executes later against pre signed rules. The real autonomy frontier.
  • Consent receipt Durable record of what the user approved. Core dispute evidence.
  • Mandate revocation Ending an agent's permission to act.
Payments
  • Scoped ephemeral credential The universal primitive. Bound to one merchant, capped at one amount, valid for minutes.
  • Agentic Token Mastercard's MDES extension binding a token to agent, merchant scope and consent policy.
  • Shared Payment Token Stripe and ACP's version of the same primitive.
  • Merchant of record The party legally selling. Stays the merchant, not the agent.
  • Token assurance The network's confidence signal about a token, carried in authorization.
Risk and measurement
  • Prompt injection Hidden text causing an agent to follow an attacker's instructions. Structurally unsolved.
  • Evidence pack Mandate, agent identity, action log, approved cart, delivery proof.
  • Action log Record of every step an agent took.
  • Found rate Share of tested prompts where an AI surfaces a brand at all.
  • Recommendation rate Share where it is actually recommended. Found is not recommended.
  • Agent conversion rate Share of agent led sessions ending in purchase.
